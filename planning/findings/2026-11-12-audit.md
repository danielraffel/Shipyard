Date: 2026-11-12. Lane: Audit (read-only). Plan: `planning/2026-11-12-autonomous-evidence-driven-operations.md`, items R9, S2, S4, O1.

## Method, sources and limits

Sources: Shipyard `origin/main` at e4f025b2 (worktree `shipyard-audit`); fleet-handbook `origin/main`; the steward log copy `/tmp/sy-plan-evidence/summary.log`; about 45 GitHub REST reads through `ghapp` and `ghread` (cached under `/tmp/audit-scripts/gh`).

Conventions. "Fact" means read from a cited source. "Inference" is marked. Nothing here was run against production.

Limits that change how to read the numbers:

- The steward log covers 2026-10-30 20:14Z to 2026-11-12 23:15Z, **13 days, not 30**. It lists only the 3 oldest blocked PRs per repo per tick, so it overweights long waits and cannot prove that a rare token never occurred. "Not observed" below means "not among the oldest 3 in any tick".
- Episode rule used: one (repo, PR, `blocked_on` token) run of observations; a gap over 45 minutes starts a new episode. Script: `/tmp/audit-scripts/ep.py` (1,614 episodes over 678 PRs; 954 episodes over 498 PRs once `required_pending` is excluded). Because of the top-3 truncation and the gap rule, the same long wait can split into several episodes; PR counts are the sturdier number.
- "Fix commits" = non-merge commits on `origin/main` since 2026-10-15 whose subject starts with `fix`, `security` or `revert`, counted by path (the files named in each row). A commit touching several rows counts in each. Whole-repo base: 965 non-merge commits, 369 of them `fix`. Union of every admission and approval path listed in section 1: **190 commits, 124 of them fixes** (20 percent of all commits, 34 percent of all fixes).
- Sources I could not read: tartci, Pulp and Vellum repositories, `pulp-planning`, GitHub labels' event history. Claims that depend on them are marked unverified.

## Findings that most change the plan (summary)

1. **Version, skill and changelog chores are the largest cost that has nothing to do with correctness, and the skill-sync map is why.** 131 "assign versions at land" commits (42 of them through bump PRs) consumed 55 CI runs, 55 review-verdict runs and 107 risk-tier runs in 12 days; `skills/shipyard` maps `src/**`, `scripts/**`, `docs/**` and `planning/**` to one 6,107-line SKILL.md, so 71 percent of PRs (136 of 191) edit a SKILL.md and 35 percent (67 of 191) need a skip trailer anyway. SKILL.md is the top conflict file: 16 of the 43 merge commits that needed a textual conflict resolution. Version files caused 0 conflicts since the cutover. (Section 3.)
2. **The changelog automation has failed 43 of 44 runs since 2026-10-31 and nothing alarmed.** `post-tag-sync` dies at `git push failed after 5 attempt(s)` (main is protected); `CHANGELOG.md` has not changed since 2026-10-31 while 32 minor versions shipped. About 240 runner minutes spent for no output. (Section 3.)
3. **The tree-level rule proposed for S2 cannot replace the carried-commit guard, because it would have missed the only incident where held code reached main.** The reviewer approved a diff that already contained the held code, so "landed diff equals approved diff" holds by construction. What the tree view can do is delete the cap and probe machinery that caused 3 of the 7 incidents (false blocks from commit-count caps) and the landed-elsewhere excuse code. Verdict: narrow, not replace. (Section 2.)
4. **Some mechanisms have never blocked anything in the log and are cheap to cut or freeze; two classes block a lot and are not safety properties.** Reviewer claims and receipts: 19 commits (9 fixes) in one burst from 2026-10-27 to 10-31, dormant since, producer not installed, 0 blocked episodes. The per-head steward handoff (`unmanaged` or `handoff_missing`): 64 episodes over 62 PRs (63 on Pulp), median 55 minutes, an author chore with no safety content. Signer, stamp and session exclusions: 0 of 17 `approval_excluded` episodes were for a signer, stamp or session reason (9 `stale_head`, 5 `untrusted_reviewer`, 3 `pr_author`). (Section 1.)
5. **Daniel stops are mostly mislabelled as owner work.** `superseded` (19 episodes, 15 PRs) tells agents `stop: true, actor: owner` although closing a superseded PR needs no human; of 16 `owner-decision` PRs, I count about 6 that truly touch secrets, signing or signer policy by title, 8 judgment calls that merged within hours, and 2 routine duplicates. There is no ledger of owner stops: the summary log keeps only the `blocked_on` token, not `next_action.actor`. (Section 4.)

Ordered by evidence strength. Each keeps A1, A2, B3 and B4.

## 2. S2: carried-commit and stacked guard

### What the guard decides today (fact: `docs/automated-draining.md`, `src/app/merge_steward_cmd/carried_commits*`)

Before any admission (steward enqueue, same-head retry, priority re-enqueue, exact-head merge, `--arm-unqueued`), on a base that requires head approval, for candidate P: read P's commits not on the base (`base...head`) and, once per pass, those of every other open PR Q. Refuse P as `carries_unapproved_commits:#Q` when P carries a commit that belongs to Q whose current head has a counted HOLD or no counted APPROVE. There is no override. Commits are excluded when:

- they are on the base, or their change is on the base tip under other shas (patch-id plus identical mode, type and blob on the pinned tip; `landed.rs`);
- Q is P's base-branch PR, is stacked on P by ancestry or by a verified `stacked:<n>` label chain, only inherits the commit, merged P's branch without being stacked on it (first-parent rule), or is a rollup of P's head;
- the older-parent rule: P opened before Q (lower number) and Q's own diff holds P's exact head.

Fail closed (`carried_commits_over_cap`, `carried_commits_unreadable`) when a listing exceeds 250 commits, probes exceed 50 per P or 100 per pass, P's graph has more than 50 roots, or any read fails. Size: `carried_commits.rs` plus `landed.rs` are 2,023 lines; tests in the same directory are 2,567 lines. 24 of 35 commits on these files since 2026-10-15 are fixes.

### Named residuals the docs admit

- R-a: P built on commits a held Q took in through a merge's second parent (Q = merge(Q.base, X) with P on X, or Q's tip re-hung as merge(Q.base, old tip)) is cleared; the docs say X "lands under P's own approval".

- R-d: the landed-elsewhere excuses (`landed.rs`, commits 4e00cc3b, 090d7da9, 11c72919, 2ad8986d, 2026-11-09 and 11-10): their commit bodies state no incident (fact: empty bodies on three of them), so their motivating case is unverified.

### The tree rule, on paper

Rule T0, as stated: the diff that lands (base to merge result) must equal the diff an eligible reviewer approved at the exact head, compared by tree, merges of the base excluded.

First observation (inference from how approval is defined): approval already names an exact head H, GitHub merges with `expectedHeadOid`, and the patch-id carry rule (B4) already proves "same diff across a rebase or main merge". T0 restates those. It cannot see anything another PR holds.

So I also evaluated T1, the strongest tree-level rule I can construct: T0 plus a content-overlap test. Let S be the hunks (or whole-file blobs) that P's diff shares with the diff of an open PR Q whose head is held or unapproved. Refuse P when S is non-empty and S is a strict subset of P's diff (P carries some of Q's work and adds its own). Allow when S equals all of P's diff (P is contained in Q, so Q merely holds P).

| Case | Current guard | T0 (strict) | T1 (T0 plus overlap) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I1 held code merged in | blocks | **misses**: approved diff contained the held hunks, so landed equals approved | blocks, if P has its own hunks beyond Q's. Degenerate case P equals Q's diff is allowed |
| I2 over-cap stacked Q | false block | no block (does not look at Q) | no block: reads tree entries, not commit lists |
| I3 2,724-commit import | false block | no block | no block, unless the file list hits GitHub's 3,000-file cap (then fail closed) |
| I4 Q merged P's branch | false block (before 5197b52c) | no block | no block: P is contained in Q |
| I5 over-cap Q and large P | false block | no block | no block, no probes |
| I6 Q introduced C, P on C, Q advanced | blocked after 551b273c | **misses** | **misses**: Q contains P, so P is "contained in Q" and exempt |
| I7 older stack parent, Q contains P | false block before c544b6ff; passes now via the age tie-break | no block | passes (same relation as I6) |
| R-a P on X taken by held Q via 2nd parent | cleared (residual) | misses | **blocks**: Q is a strict subset of P, so P carries Q's X plus its own |
| R-b `stacked:P` label, P reset onto Q's held commits | cleared (residual) | misses | **blocks**: the label is irrelevant to content |
| R-c caps and unreadable | fails closed, 14 episodes / 8 PRs | none | far fewer triggers (no commit caps); the 3,000-file cap and API errors remain |
| R-d landed elsewhere under other shas | `landed.rs`, 390 lines | n/a | free: content already on the tip is not in diff(base..P), so no excuse code |
| cherry-pick or squash of Q's held commit into P | not detected (new sha) | misses | detected if the patch-id matches; missed if conflict resolution changed it |

### Strongest counterexamples against the tree rule

1. **I6 and I7 are the same relation at the content level.** In both, Q contains all of P's diff. In I6 the shared commit C came from the held Q and P must be refused; in I7 it came from P and P must be admitted. A hunk cannot say which PR first contained it. The current guard separates them with an ancestry rule plus "P opened first" (lower PR number), which is itself a proxy. T1 gives the wrong answer on I6 unless it adds the same proxy, or better evidence of first appearance (the timestamp of the first push that contained the hunk, from GitHub events). So the tree view does not remove the hardest case; it only reaches it by a different path.
2. **I1 itself.** T0 passes it by construction. The breach was not "the landed bytes differ from the approved bytes"; it was "the approved bytes contained code that another review had condemned". That is a property across PRs, so any rule that looks only at P versus its approval cannot see it.
3. **Identical-hunk coincidence causes false blocks under T1.** Generated or lock files where P and a held Q both regenerate the same lines: Pulp's `test/ctest_script_inputs.json` (75 commits in 7 days per lessons.md, entry 8), the `SURFACE_INVENTORY_VERSION` counter, `Cargo.lock`. S is non-empty, P has other hunks, Q is held: P is refused with no real carry. Mitigation exists (`generated_paths` config) but is per-repo configuration.
4. **Laundering by edit.** A cherry-pick with a conflict resolution, or a renamed variable, changes the patch-id, so T1 misses it. The ancestry guard misses a cherry-pick too (new sha), so this is no regression, but it means T1's gain is partial. Threat model here is accident, not adversary.
5. **Cost.** T1 needs a diff for every open Q per pass. GitHub omits patches for large files, so a hunk-level patch-id is unavailable for exactly the large cases; blob equality per path is exact and cheap but misses partial overlap in a shared file.

### Verdict: narrow, do not replace

- Keep the property check (held or unreviewed work does not ride another PR). The tree rule does not replace it (counterexamples 1 and 2).
- Replace the machinery that produces false blocks from counting commits: the 250-commit listing cap, the 50-commit and 100-probe budgets, the root probes, and the landed-elsewhere excuse. Decide overlap from trees (per-path blob equality first, hunk patch-id where the patch is present), with the containment exemption. Evidence that this is where the cost is: I2, I3 and I5 are cap artifacts (3 of 7 incidents), 14 `over_cap` or `unreadable` episodes, and `landed.rs` plus its 3 fix commits. R-a and R-b become blocks instead of named residuals.
- Keep a first-appearance tie-break for the I6 versus I7 relation, and say plainly that it is a proxy (today: PR number). Better evidence is the push event time of the shared commit.
- Net on paper: the guard stays but loses the cap, probe and landed code paths. I did not measure how many lines that removes beyond `landed.rs` (390).

Unverified: the tree overlap rule was reasoned, not prototyped. A cheap check before building: replay the 20 carry-family PRs and the 7 incidents offline and compare verdicts.

### What each costs, in work

Fact, from the GitHub Actions API for branch `release/version-at-land` (329 runs): the 42 bump PRs ran 55 `CI` runs (477 summed run-wall minutes; 13 more than PRs because the branch is rebuilt and force-pushed on each landing), 55 `Review verdict` runs (48 of them re-attempts), 55 `Versioning & Skill-Sync`, 107 `Risk tier`, 55 `Sandbox E2E` (skipped). That is 55 of the 300 `ci.yml` pull-request runs since 2026-10-31 (18 percent). Each PR also needs an exact-head review pass: up to 42 passes (the version-only fast path, `tools/fleet-pass/version_only.py`, is in shadow; fact: commit dfc640b4 "classify version-bump-only diffs and check them in shadow").

Each landing also triggers `version-at-land.yml` (167 runs since 10-31, mostly no-ops) and `auto-release.yml` (166 runs). Each bump merge creates a tag, and each tag a `release.yml` run: 47 runs, 12 failed, 3 cancelled, median 11.4 min, 995 summed wall minutes. That release volume, not the bump commit, is the larger cost, and 32 of 88 releases never completed.

Changelog: `post-tag-sync` is a cost with no product: about 44 runs times 5.5 minutes, 43 failures, and `CHANGELOG.md` last changed 2026-10-31 (commit 4968c373, v0.382.1) while the CLI moved to v0.414.0.

Conflicts (fact, recomputed with `git merge-tree` on the parents of every merge commit since 10-15): 43 of 335 merge commits reproduce a textual conflict. 16 of those 43 conflict in `skills/shipyard/SKILL.md` (11) or `skills/ci/SKILL.md` (8). 0 conflict in a version file (`Cargo.toml`, `Cargo.lock`, `plugin.json`, `marketplace.json`); 1 in `CHANGELOG.md`. Version-at-land did what it was built for. Using `--cc` on the same merges, `skills/shipyard/SKILL.md` was touched on both sides in 111 of 148 merges that touched anything on both sides, which is overlap, not conflict.

### Why a planning-only or docs-only PR trips skill-sync (confirmed in code and by running the gate)

`scripts/skill_path_map.json`, skill `shipyard`, paths: `src/**`, `scripts/**`, `.github/workflows/**`, `tools/sandbox-e2e/**`, **`docs/**`**, **`planning/**`**, `skills/shipyard/**`. `compute_findings` in `scripts/skill_sync_check.py` makes any changed path matching a skill's patterns a finding unless that skill's `SKILL.md` is in the diff or a `Skill-Update: skip skill=<name>` trailer exists in some commit of the range. Skill `ci` maps `src/**`, `Cargo.*`, `commands/**`, `agents/**`, `hooks/**`, `.claude-plugin/**`, `.github/workflows/**` and five release scripts, so every `src/` change needs two SKILL.md edits or two skips. I ran `scripts/skill_sync_check.py --mode=report --base origin/main --head HEAD` on this audit's own commit (one new file under `planning/findings/`): result `[shipyard] SKILL.md NOT updated`, skill `ci` not triggered, exit demanded a `Skill-Update: skip skill=shipyard` trailer. Side finding: `python3 -I` cannot run the script (isolated mode drops the script directory, so `from version_bump_check import resolve_config` fails with ModuleNotFoundError); it needs plain `python3`. Second footgun, also reproduced: with the bypass trailer in its own paragraph above a blank line and the `Co-Authored-By:` attribution line after it, the gate still failed; `git interpret-trailers --parse` reads only the last paragraph, so the attribution footer hid the `Skill-Update:` trailer. It passed once the two lines were adjacent (`bypassed (planning-only audit findings)`). Any agent that appends the required attribution as a separate paragraph silently disables every bypass trailer. The gate already reads trailers from every commit in the range (`git_range_trailers`), so a trailer is a commit message, not a follow-up commit; the follow-up commits are agents choosing to write SKILL.md text instead.

### Options to cut the volume

| Option | Cuts | What it would break |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a. Remove `docs/**`, `planning/**`, `scripts/**` from skill `shipyard`; narrow `src/**` to the subsystems a skill actually documents (steward, ship, ci) | most of the 136 SKILL.md edits and 67 skip trailers; most SKILL.md conflicts | drift in SKILL.md for unmapped areas; requires a rule for who owns docs of un-mapped paths (the map's self-check requires every `skills/` dir to appear, not every path) |
| b. Split the 11,000-line SKILL.md files into per-subsystem files, map each to its paths | conflicts (two PRs rarely touch the same subsystem file) | skill loading cost and discoverability for agents; a migration |
| c. Make skill-sync advisory (`--mode=hint`) for `docs/**` and `planning/**`, hard only for `src/app/**` public surface | planning and docs friction at once | weaker guarantee that CLI changes update skills |
| d. Stop opening a bump PR per landing: cut a release on demand or on a schedule (for example daily), assign versions then | 42 bump PRs, 55 CI runs, 55 review runs, about 88 releases down to roughly one per cadence, and the 32 incomplete drafts | `shipyard update` and fleet-update see fewer versions; hotfix latency rises to the cadence unless a manual trigger exists; plugin consumers need a committed version bump to receive changes from git, so the plugin surface needs its own rule |
| e. Fast-path the bump PR: admit a version-files-only diff on the signed bot author with the CI that matters (inference: skip macOS lanes through changed-surface selection) | review passes and CI minutes per bump PR | trust anchor is the bot identity and its key (the docs already warn: keep the signing key off agent hosts); wrong fast-path is an unreviewed-content path |

Recommendation (inference): (a) plus (g) now; measure (d) after one week of (a), because the release count, not the bump commit, is the large cost, and (d) changes what "released" means for fleet hosts.
